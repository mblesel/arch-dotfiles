# General web research

## Preferred sources

* Relevant official organizations, government agencies, public records, reports, and other primary documents — establish what an organization said or did.
* Reputable reporting and specialist publications — find context, interviews, and independent scrutiny; trace pivotal claims to original evidence where possible.
* [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/) and topic-specific communities — find firsthand experiences, terminology, and questions to investigate.
* [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/) — discussion of technology and related business or science topics.
* [Dev.to](https://dev.to/), [Medium](https://medium.com/), and independent blogs — find explanations and case studies; check the author's evidence and expertise.
* Public Discord discussions and [X](https://x.com/) posts — find firsthand announcements or accounts; look for a stable, directly accessible link when citing them.

## Search and assess

* Vary names, synonyms, dates, and geographical context. Search a relevant site directly when broad results are noisy.
* For current events, confirm dates and seek the original statement, document, or data behind a report.
* For comparisons, state the criteria and look for independent evidence, real-world experiences, and meaningful trade-offs.
* Attribute community accounts to their authors; do not present a few posts as representative consensus.
